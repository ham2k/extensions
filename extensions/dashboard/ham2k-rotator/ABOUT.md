See where your antenna points, and turn it with a click. The panel draws your rotator on a map of the world centred on your station, where every straight line out from the middle is the path your signal takes.

## Turn it

Click anywhere on the map to turn the rotator there, or drag the heading arrow. Type a call in the logging fields and the station shows as a red dot; click it to turn straight to it. While the rotator turns, its old heading fades and a dashed arrow shows where it is going, with a Stop button beside it.

## Look around

Scroll to zoom in, and drag your station to pan. State and province lines appear as you get closer, and the night side of the Earth is shaded with the twilight bands around it.

## Set it up

Pick the rotator in the panel's settings, or show several on one map, each in its own colour and with its own label. Tell it what the antenna is — a rotatable beam, or a 4-square or 8-direction array that only points one of a few ways — and it draws the beam to match. Choose a theme, and set a location if the map should centre somewhere other than your operation.

Add your rotator under Station Configuration first. Works with Hamlib's rotctld, PstRotator, Yaesu GS-232 interfaces and Green Heron Everyware.
